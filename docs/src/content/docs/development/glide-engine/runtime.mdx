**Scope.** Architecturally relevant scenarios: a single glide, a preview, the
picker, an external engine, a chained stroke and a two-thumb word. **Not
covered:** drawing the trail and the pill, which are view code.

**Verified against** the `v0.6.4` base plus the uncommitted glide-engine work at commit `8f61dd563`, on 2026-11-05. This is not release `v0.6.5`; see the [sync note](/development/glide-engine/#version-and-sync).

## One swipe, from touch to commit

<Mermaid
	caption="The lift decodes the stroke again. The preview is not reused, but it is not ignored either: the preview gate makes the shown word win when the finished stroke still ranks it close to its own leader."
	chart={`
sequenceDiagram
  autonumber
  actor User
  participant Grid as Key grid
  participant Service as WMKeyboardService
  participant Engine as SuggestionEngine
  participant Native as wm-engine
  participant Store as Learning stores

  User->>Grid: pointer down
  Grid->>Grid: sample and arbitrate tap, flick or glide
  User->>Grid: drag across letters
  Grid->>Service: onGesturePreview(path)
  Service->>Engine: glide(path, keys, sources)
  Engine->>Native: glideDecode or ctcDecode
  Native-->>Engine: ranked candidates
  Engine-->>Service: GlideReading
  Service-->>Grid: preview words and pill
  User->>Grid: lift
  Grid->>Service: onGesture(path, GlideVerdict)
  Service->>Engine: glide(path, keys, sources)
  Engine->>Native: decode with the same look-ahead
  Native-->>Engine: ranked candidates
  Engine-->>Service: GlideReading
  Service->>Service: choose leader or picker word, apply case
  Service->>Store: learn word, shape, outcome
  Service->>Grid: commit word, space and strip
`}
/>

The lift path checks `glideAllowed` before any decode. A typing test, a capture
target, a secure field, or an unavailable word list can stop the stroke before it
reaches the engine. The generation counter is incremented on a cancel and on a
new gesture, and the preview consumer drops any result whose generation no longer
matches. That is the guard against a slow preview overwriting the strip after the
finger has already committed.

## Preview and the ambiguity picker

<Mermaid
	caption="The preview is a loop. The picker is a deadline inside that loop: the service publishes choices on every reading, and the dwell clock decides when the words appear."
	chart={`
sequenceDiagram
  autonumber
  actor User
  participant Grid as Key grid
  participant Service as WMKeyboardService
  participant Gate as GlidePreviewGate
  participant Dwell as GlideDwell
  participant Engine as SuggestionEngine

  loop while the finger moves
    Grid->>Service: onGesturePreview(path)
    Service->>Engine: decode
    Engine-->>Service: words, scores, closeCall
    Service->>Gate: steady(words, scores)
    Gate-->>Service: shown word and side slots
    Service-->>Grid: publish strip, pill, key words
    Service->>Dwell: sample stillness
  end
  alt close call or hold-to-ask
    Dwell-->>Service: timeout
    Service-->>Grid: open picker with choices
    User->>Grid: slide onto a word or down to cancel
    Grid->>Service: GlideVerdict.Word or Cancel
  else no picker
    User->>Grid: lift
    Grid->>Service: GlideVerdict.Leader
  end
  Service->>Engine: final decode
  Service->>Service: Gate.commit(shownAtLift, final words)
`}
/>

Three rules keep the preview honest.

- The steadiest word on screen wins a lift only while the finished stroke still
  ranks it within the tier's margin. Past that margin the stroke has become a
  different word and the screen was merely behind.
- A word that drops out of the reading is replaced at once, whatever its age.
- A side slot is display only. `GlidePreviewGate.commit` never reads it, so a
  held neighbour cannot change what the lift types.

A cancel in the picker types nothing and teaches nothing. It retires the preview
and restores the strip to the field's state before the stroke.

## External engine fallback

<Mermaid
	caption="The chosen external engine gets the first answer. A null answer is a deliberate refusal, not an error, and the built-in beam takes the stroke."
	chart={`
sequenceDiagram
  autonumber
  participant Service as WMKeyboardService
  participant Engine as SuggestionEngine
  participant External as NeuralGlide or LatinImeGlide
  participant Beam as GlideBeam
  participant Native as wm-engine

  Service->>Service: glideEngineNow()
  alt FUTO Swipe selected, English, model loaded
    Service->>External: NeuralGlide
  else Google library selected, imported, unlocked
    Service->>External: LatinImeGlide
  else otherwise
    Service->>Beam: no external engine
  end
  Service->>Engine: externalGlide = engine
  Service->>Engine: glide(path, keys, sources)
  Engine->>External: decode(path, keys, sources)
  alt engine can read the stroke
    External-->>Engine: candidates
  else null
    External-->>Engine: null
    Engine->>Beam: decode(path, keys, sources)
    Beam->>Native: glideDecode
    Native-->>Beam: candidates
    Beam-->>Engine: candidates
  end
`}
/>

The service selects FUTO only for English on a QWERTY grid. The Google engine
returns `null` until its dictionary for the language is built; the FUTO engine
returns `null` for a non-QWERTY frame, a short stroke, or a missing model or
runtime. Every `null` sends the stroke to the built-in decoder, so the user sees
a word rather than an error. The canonical rules are on
[Languages and engines](/development/glide-engine/languages-and-engines/#engines).

## Chained words

<Mermaid
	caption="A chained stroke is split at the spacebar. Only the last segment may guess ahead, because only it ends where the finger lifted."
	chart={`
flowchart TB
  A["Gesture ends"] --> B{"Crossed the spacebar<br/>and multi-word is on?"}
  B -- yes --> C["Split into segments"]
  C --> D["Read open head and open tail"]
  D --> E["Decode each segment"]
  E --> F{"Early segment empty?"}
  F -- yes --> G["Re-decode with a closed end"]
  F -- no --> H["Commit each word in order"]
  G --> H
  B -- no --> I["Single-word path"]
`}
/>

`GlideStroke.openRun` measures how many samples at an end are a walk from or to
the spacebar. Those samples cost nothing and may hold the first or last letter
anywhere on the run. The last segment is the only one that may guess ahead,
because the earlier segments end where the finger turned into the spacebar, not
where it lifted.

## Commit semantics

| Event | What happens | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Lift on the leader | The shown word wins if it is within the steadiness margin; otherwise the finished stroke's leader wins | `GlidePreviewGate.commit` |
| Lift on a picker word | The picked word commits in place of the leader and is treated identically afterwards | `GlideVerdict.Word` |
| Lift in the picker's cancel zone | Nothing commits, nothing is learned, previews retire | `cancelGlide` |
| Space after the word | The keyboard adds it, and a following punctuation mark takes it back | `GlideSpace` |
| Space press right after | Confirms the typed space instead of doubling it | `commitGestureSpace` |
| Backspace right after | Removes the whole glided word and its space, unless the setting is off | `armRevertGuard` |
| Alternate tapped | Replaces the word; `GlideOutcomes` learns the pair | Suggestion path |
| Undo plus retry | A deep decode runs without the vocabulary cap and with a wider pool | `glideDeepRetry` |
| Word settles | The shape, the word and the outcome evidence are learned | `LearningBuffer` |

Shape learning is deliberately after settlement, not at commit. A word the user
backspaces or replaces teaches no shape; the correction or undo is still recorded
by `GlideOutcomes`. A word they leave alone settles, and only then does its shape
reach `GlideShapeStore`.
